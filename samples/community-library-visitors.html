1. Discover

Here's what we found.

Source: S1: Pew Research Center (2013), "Part 4: Parents and Libraries" (US national survey, Oct-Nov 2012)

Research summary: In this US survey, 27% of parents said they know all or most of their library's services and programs, 47% know some, and 25% know not much or nothing. Parents with a child under 12 were less likely than parents with no children under 12 to say they know all or most (24% vs. 36%). Mothers said they know more than fathers (32% vs. 21% all or most), and lower-income parents more than those making $50,000 or more (31% vs. 21%). (S1) The rest of the problem (the quiet image, scheduling, budgets) was not covered by this one source and is marked as hypothesis.

3. Develop

Here's the set of ideas.

C1: Parents of young children do not know most of what their library offers.

  • C1-I1: Pediatrician "prescription" card listing free library events for kids 0-5
  • C1-I2: One-page "What's free for your kid" menu handed out at kindergarten registration
  • C1-I3: Welcome-baby library card mailed with birth-certificate paperwork via county clerk
  • C1-I4: "Did you know?" stickers on every children's book checked out
  • C1-I5: Library staff table at the local farmers market on Saturdays

C4: Event news sits on library websites and paper flyers that busy parents never see.

  • C4-I1: Weekly 3-line SMS digest of kids' events (opt-in by texting a keyword)
  • C4-I2: Post events in local parent Facebook groups and Nextdoor every Monday
  • C4-I3: QR posters at daycare pickup doors linking to this week's events
  • C4-I4: Shared Google Calendar parents can subscribe to in one tap
  • C4-I5: Partner with local coffee shop to print events on cup sleeves

C3: Parents fear a noisy toddler will be shushed in a 'quiet' library, so they stay away.

  • C3-I1: "Loud Hour": a weekly hour where noise, crawling and crying are welcome
  • C3-I2: Signs at the door: "Noisy kids welcome in this zone"
  • C3-I3: Toddler-proof play corner with soft mats and duplo blocks
  • C3-I4: Short video tour by a local parent showing a toddler playing in the library
  • C3-I5: Stroller parking and coffee for parents during story time

C2: Fathers know far less about library services than mothers, so half of each family is out of the loop.

  • C2-I1: Saturday "Dads & Donuts" story time
  • C2-I2: Add library events to youth-sports league emails coaches send to parents
  • C2-I3: Library-of-things tool demo for parents (drills, telescopes) on Saturday mornings
  • C2-I4: Hand library cards to both parents at sign-up, not just one
  • C2-I5: Video-game and board-game night for parent-kid pairs

C6: Free tools (library-of-things, museum passes, makerspace) are invisible to families.

  • C6-I1: "Borrow it free" display in the front window with price tags showing money saved
  • C6-I2: Monthly "free stuff for families" list posted to the town website
  • C6-I3: Museum-pass raffle at story time
  • C6-I4: Kids' birthday party kits (games, decorations) to borrow
  • C6-I5: Cooking-gear and baby-gear lending shelf

5. Pretotypes

Here's the test plan.

K1-P1 (K1 Back-to-School Library Menu) - recommended first test

  • Hypothesis: Parents of kids 4-6 will want a list of free library offerings if it reaches them through school.
  • Test: Hand 200 QR flyers ("Claim your family's free library menu") at kindergarten registration and preschool pickup.
  • Success: At least 30 of 200 flyers scanned (15%) by Thu Nov 19, 2026
  • Deadline: Thu Nov 19, 2026
  • Go / no-go: Go if 30+ scans by Nov 19; no-go if fewer than 30.

Materials for K1-P1 (prepared only, not sent):

  • Flyer front: "Your kid's library is free, loud-friendly, and full of surprises. Scan to claim your family's free library menu."
  • Landing page headline: "What's free for your kid at [Library Name]"
  • Landing page body: "Story times, museum passes, free e-books and tools to borrow. Enter your email and we'll send the one-page menu." Button: "Send me the menu"
  • Thank-you note (fake door): "Thanks! The menu is being finished and arrives next week."

K1-P2 (K1 Back-to-School Library Menu)

  • Hypothesis: Fathers will click library info when it arrives inside youth-sports emails.
  • Test: Ask 3 youth-league coaches to add a 2-line library note with a tracked link to their weekly parent email.
  • Success: At least 15 unique clicks from about 150 recipients (10%) by Thu Dec 3, 2026
  • Deadline: Thu Dec 3, 2026
  • Go / no-go: Go if 15+ clicks by Dec 3; no-go if fewer than 15.

K2-P1 (K2 Loud & Free Saturdays) - recommended first test

  • Hypothesis: Young parents will sign up for a library hour where noisy kids are explicitly welcome.
  • Test: Post a "Loud Hour Saturday" announcement with an RSVP form in 3 local parent Facebook groups and Nextdoor.
  • Success: At least 20 family RSVPs by Thu Nov 26, 2026
  • Deadline: Thu Nov 26, 2026
  • Go / no-go: Go if 20+ RSVPs by Nov 26; no-go if fewer than 20.

Materials for K2-P1 (prepared only, not sent):

  • Post text: "Shh-free zone! Bring your little ones to Loud Hour this Saturday, 10-11 am at [Library Name]. Crying, crawling and giggling welcome. Free. RSVP here: [form link]"
  • RSVP form questions: 1) Your first name; 2) Number and ages of kids; 3) Which Saturday works best?; 4) What would make you come back?
  • Reply script for comments: "Yes, really loud is okay! It's our hour for families with small kids."

K2-P2 (K2 Loud & Free Saturdays)

  • Hypothesis: Showing money saved makes families notice free tools.
  • Test: Put a "Borrow it free" front-window display with price tags and a QR code to the lending list.
  • Success: At least 25 QR scans in 2 weeks by Thu Dec 10, 2026
  • Deadline: Thu Dec 10, 2026
  • Go / no-go: Go if 25+ scans by Dec 10; no-go if fewer than 25.
